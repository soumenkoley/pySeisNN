# Surface-to-depth seismic attenuation

This chapter documents `getSeisAttn.ipynb`. The notebook estimates the
frequency-dependent displacement attenuation between the surface and an
underground point for several stochastic seismic-field models.

The calculation is motivated by geological-model validation for Newtonian
noise. Before a one-dimensional geology is used to predict underground NN, it
should reproduce relevant seismic observations at the candidate site. One key
observation is the measured change in displacement amplitude between the
surface and detector depth. This notebook tests a proposed model against that
constraint.

Attenuation agreement is necessary but not sufficient. The notebook also
compares the model's fundamental Rayleigh-wave dispersion curve with the
observed site dispersion. A model that matches surface-to-depth attenuation
but fails the dispersion comparison should not be treated as fully validated.

## What the notebook calculates

Displacement is evaluated at the same horizontal coordinate at two depths:

```python
zList = np.array((0.0, 250.0))
gridX = np.array((0.0,))
gridY = np.array((0.0,))
```

The two physical locations are therefore:

```text
surface:       (0, 0,   0 m depth)
underground:   (0, 0, 250 m depth)
```

For every frequency and Cartesian component, the notebook accumulates
displacement power over stochastic realizations:

```text
P_surface(f) = sum_r |u_surface^(r)(f)|^2
P_depth(f)   = sum_r |u_depth^(r)(f)|^2
```

It defines attenuation as:

```text
A(f) = sqrt(P_surface(f) / P_depth(f))
     = RMS surface amplitude / RMS underground amplitude.
```

This convention means:

- `A > 1`: displacement is smaller underground than at the surface;
- `A = 1`: equal RMS amplitude at the two depths; and
- `A < 1`: the underground RMS amplitude is larger.

The ratio is calculated separately for x, y, and z displacement.

## Scientific use in NN model selection

The geological profile determines wave propagation, surface-wave depth decay,
reflections, conversions, interference, and attenuation. Those effects also
determine the displacement field used by the NN integral. A model that produces
the wrong surface-to-depth transfer may give an unrealistic underground NN
prediction even if its elastic parameters appear plausible individually.

A practical model-screening workflow is:

1. propose a layered velocity, density, and quality-factor profile;
2. choose a physically motivated stochastic-source distribution;
3. simulate surface and underground displacement with this notebook;
4. compare all three attenuation components with observed percentile bands;
5. compare the model Rayleigh dispersion with observed dispersion;
6. revise the geology or source model if either comparison fails; and
7. use accepted models for the subsequent NN ensemble calculation.

Agreement should be assessed across the analysis band, not at a single
frequency.

## Qseis database generation

For `if_full_field = True`, the notebook prepares the two required receiver
depths with:

```python
splitAll = createDB.createFinalInputsTD(config, zList)
```

It then executes all Qseis jobs and assembles these components:

```text
fh-2.tz  fh-2.tr  fh-2.tt  fz-2.tz  fz-2.tr
```

`assembleQseisRuns` is called with `onlySplitAll=False` and
`minVel = 100 m/s`. Only the surface and 250 m receiver planes are required for
this comparison.

For analytic body- or Rayleigh-wave cases, Qseis is not run and
`onlySplitAll=True` produces geometry metadata only.

## Source generation

Every realization generates 100 simultaneous sources or plane waves.

For full-field configurations, `genAmbSrc` draws force positions, azimuths,
amplitudes, and phases. With `decoupled_hv = True`, horizontal and vertical
force amplitudes and phases are independent. `scale_vh = 1` introduces no
systematic vertical/horizontal bias.

Internal sources are distributed within the simulation domain. Ring sources
are placed between their configured `r_1` and `r_2`, typically 6000–6500 m for
the supplied ring cases. The ring configurations therefore use
`xmax_gf = 13000 m`.

Random seeds and source metadata are not retained, so the exact ensemble is not
reproducible without adding seed control.

## Depth conventions

The user-facing depth list is positive downward. The analytic functions use
Cartesian z, so the notebook calls them with:

```python
# surface
z = zList[0]

# 250 m underground
z = -zList[1]
```

This conversion is applied to both body- and Rayleigh-wave depth calculations.
The Qseis GF interface uses positive receiver depth and therefore receives
`zList[0]` and `zList[1]` directly.

## Analytic body-wave check

The body-wave branch generates stochastic isotropic P and S plane waves with:

```python
eP = 1/3
eS = 2/3
```

An infinite plane wave does not geometrically decay between the two points.
For an isotropic ensemble in a homogeneous full space, the expected RMS
surface/depth ratio is therefore approximately one for all three components.
The notebook overlays `A = 1` as the theoretical check.

This case validates the estimator and Monte Carlo convergence; it does not
represent free-surface amplification in a layered half-space.

## Analytic Rayleigh-wave check

For `if_ray = True`, `pysurf96` provides the fundamental Rayleigh phase
velocity. The same stochastic plane-wave realization is evaluated at the
surface and at `z = -250 m`.

`rayleigh_transfer_function` returns the horizontal and vertical depth
transfer magnitudes, conventionally denoted here by `Th` and `Tz`. Because the
notebook plots surface divided by depth, its theoretical curves are:

```text
horizontal attenuation = 1 / Th
vertical attenuation   = 1 / Tz
```

The same horizontal prediction is plotted for x and y under the isotropic
Rayleigh-wave assumption.

## Qseis full-field calculation

For each stochastic force realization, `computeFullDispF` is called once at
the surface and once at 250 m depth. The same source metadata drive both calls,
so their ratio measures propagation between the two levels rather than a
difference between independent source ensembles.

`on_fly_nn` must be false: this notebook needs displacement at the receiver,
not an NN-reduced quantity. Temporary shared-memory fields are cleaned after
their squared magnitudes have been accumulated.

The Qseis result includes the response allowed by the configured 1D model,
including surface waves, body waves, reflections, conversions, attenuation,
and source-distribution effects.

## Interpreting model agreement

A candidate model should not be accepted solely because its simulated median
lies somewhere inside a broad observed band. Consider:

- agreement across x, y, and z components;
- frequency dependence across the complete measured band;
- observed uncertainty and percentile width;
- convergence with source and realization counts;
- sensitivity to internal versus ring source distributions;
- agreement with observed Rayleigh dispersion;
- plausible layer velocities, densities, thicknesses, and quality factors;
  and
- numerical convergence of the Qseis database.

Mismatch can arise from the geological model, the assumed source distribution,
the horizontal/vertical force mixture, insufficient ensemble convergence, or
numerical setup. The attenuation comparison constrains their combined effect;
it does not uniquely invert the geology.

## Convergence checks

Vary and test:

- `n_src` and `n_rea`;
- internal versus ring source distributions;
- ring radius and GF horizontal extent;
- horizontal/vertical source weighting;
- Qseis receiver-distance and depth sampling;
- `t_max`, `n_samp`, and the arrival-window `minVel`;
- attenuation quality factors; and
- small perturbations of layer thicknesses and velocities.

The ratio can become unstable where underground power is extremely small.
Inspect `dispDepthAllRea` and avoid interpreting divisions dominated by
numerical noise.
